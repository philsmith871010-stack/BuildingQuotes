Three builders. Three prices. None of them what you pay.

You get quotes that differ by forty thousand pounds and no way to tell which one is honest. Then the job starts, the ground turns out to be what it always was, and the price moves anyway.

−0.400Topsoil

Most quotes are priced off a drawing and a hunch.

Nobody has measured your house. Nobody has looked at your soil. The number is a rate per square metre and an optimistic assumption, and the gap between that and the real job is where extras come from.

−1.000Where most quotes stop

A metre down is the standard answer.

It is also the cheapest one to assume. On free-draining ground it is usually right. On the heavy clay under most of Essex, with anything thirsty growing nearby, it is not even close.

−2.650Because of one oak

An oak eight metres away costs twenty‑one thousand pounds.

Its roots draw water out of clay, the clay shrinks, and the footing has to go below the reach of it. That is not an extra. It was always going to be true, and Datum tells you on the first screen rather than in month three.

Six stages, in this order.

Price it here

Ninety seconds, no site visit, no salesman. A range and the full breakdown behind it.

Say go

We brief the architect and the structural engineer. You do not chase anybody or explain the job twice.

Drawings

Measured survey, plans, planning or permitted development, then structural design for your actual ground.

The accurate price

Drawings come back into Datum and the job is re-priced off the real design. This is the figure the contract is written against.

Tender

That price goes to our vetted builders. They accept it or they pass. Nobody undercuts anybody, so nobody claws it back later.

Build

JCT contract. We value the work every two weeks, and the builder invoices what we have valued. Not before.

Why the price holds

The extras problem, solved by the contract.

Nothing added without us

Extras come through Datum, not through you

If something genuinely unforeseen turns up, we approve it. A builder cannot bill you for something they should have allowed for, because we priced it and we know what was allowed.

Paid for work done

We measure it before you pay for it

Every fortnight we value what has actually been built. The invoice follows the valuation. That is how commercial jobs have always worked.

Council, always

Local authority building control

Never a private inspector chosen by the builder. The people signing the work off answer to the council, not to whoever is paying them.

Contingency on the face of it

Held, not hidden — and returned if unspent

Every honest job carries a contingency. Most quotes bury it in the rates. Ours is a line you can see, and what is not spent comes back to you.

For builders

Accept work. Don't bid for it.

You are not in a race to the bottom with four other firms. The job arrives measured and priced, with a ten per cent margin already in it. You look at it and you either take it or you don't.

Because the price was set before you saw it, nobody wins by shaving it, and nobody needs to make it back on extras. The client already knows what you are earning, which is a much easier conversation than the one where they find out.

Straight answers

Three things we would rather you heard from us.

On the estimate

It is an estimate, and it says so

The figure is a range, and it stays a range until an architect has measured your house and an engineer has looked at your ground. It is not a quotation and nothing is fixed until there is a contract.

On the obvious question

Yes — we price the job and we take a cut of it

So the rates are published, the breakdown is the same one your builder sees, and every valuation is evidenced with photographs and measurements. If our five per cent is on the page in front of you, we cannot quietly grow it.

On what is missing

What this version does not know yet

Party wall awards, site access, drains under the footprint, roof glazing, heating and electrical work, decoration and floor finishes, and which planning route you need. All of them move the number, and all of them are being added.

On the figures

These rates are a first pass

The build rate, the door rate and the foundation table are starting figures for a working demonstration. They are being replaced with a full quantity surveyor's schedule.
